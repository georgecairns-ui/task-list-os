# Step 4: get to know the business

Goal: fill in the `context/` notes so everything you do afterwards is specific to this business. This is what makes your suggestions sound like a colleague's rather than a stranger's.

## 4.1 Use what you already have

Before asking anything, gather what you can:

- Anything found in step 1 (a document describing the business, a website address in their email signature).
- Their connected apps: the email signature, the business name in the accounts app, client names in the CRM or unpaid invoices.
- Their website, if they gave you one and you can read web pages.

Don't present guesses as facts. Use them to skip questions you can already answer ("I can see from your invoices that your main clients are Marlow Dental and Greenway Café, so I'll skip that one").

## 4.2 Ask the questions

One friendly message, numbered, skipping anything you already know. Tell them short answers are fine.

1. In a sentence or two, what does the business do and who for?
2. What do you sell, and roughly what does a typical job or order cost? (Skip this if you'd rather not say.)
3. Who else is involved? Team, freelancers, a virtual assistant, your accountant. First names and what they do.
4. Who are the clients, customers or suppliers you deal with most?
5. What matters most over the next 3 months? (For example more clients, getting paid faster, less admin, launching something.)
6. When do you usually work? Days and rough hours.
7. Anything you do every week or month that I should remind you about? (Invoicing, payroll, VAT, social posts, stock orders.)
8. How do you like to come across in emails: more formal or more relaxed? Anything you'd never say?

## 4.3 Learn how they write (optional, recommended)

Offer this once:

> If you're happy for me to, I can read 10 to 20 of your recent sent emails to learn how you write. Then any draft I prepare will sound like you. I'll only keep notes on your style, not copies of the emails.

If they agree and email is connected, follow `skills/write-like-me/SKILL.md` in "learn" mode. If not, use their answer to question 8 and any examples they paste.

## 4.4 Write up the context notes

Fill in each file in `context/`. Replace the template text, and change the status line at the top to `Status: filled in on <date>`.

| File | What goes in |
|---|---|
| `context/business.md` | What the business does, for whom, what it sells, price range, location, website, how long it's been going |
| `context/people.md` | Team and helpers (who does what), key clients, customers and suppliers, how formal to be with each |
| `context/priorities.md` | The 3 month priorities, in their words, plus anything you learned about what keeps them up at night |
| `context/routines.md` | Working days and hours, and every recurring job with how often and when |
| `context/voice.md` | How they write: greetings, sign-offs, tone by audience, phrases they use, things they never say |

Keep each file short and factual. Bullet points are fine. No marketing language.

## 4.5 Check it with them

Show a 5 to 8 line summary of what you wrote, not the files themselves:

> Here's what I've noted. Fern & Finch is a 2 person branding and web studio in Bristol, mostly working with local hospitality and healthcare businesses. Priya freelances for you on design. Your main clients right now are Marlow Dental and Greenway Café. The big goal for the next 3 months is getting paid faster. You invoice on the 1st of each month and VAT is quarterly. In emails you're friendly but brief, and you sign off "Cheers, Sam". Anything I've got wrong?

Fix anything they correct. Tick step 4 in `setup/progress.md`.
